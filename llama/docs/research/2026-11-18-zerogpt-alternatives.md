# ZeroGPT alternatives: local and open-source options

Research snapshot: 2026-11-18. Historical assessment, not an installed or benchmarked configuration. The recommendations below distinguish published evidence, vendor claims, and deployment inferences. No detector was executed during this review.

## Recommendation

For a straightforward private local pilot, start with **Desklib** and compare **ModernBERT-Detect** as a smaller, unrestricted baseline. **MELD** is the most promising recent research candidate found if its download conditions are acceptable. **Tropa-mini** is another practical CPU candidate, but its own subgroup results make it unsuitable for confident judgments about learner writing. **Fast-DetectGPT** and **Binoculars** are valuable research methods with much higher resource costs. These rankings concern suitability for a pilot, not proven superiority on the user's documents.

For a hosted product, start a comparison with **Pangram** and **GPTZero**. For an organization requiring private infrastructure and vendor support, investigate **Copyleaks Cloud Private Hubs**. None of these hosted recommendations means that their scores establish authorship.

## Local shortlist

| **[MELD](https://huggingface.co/anon-review-meld-2026/meld)** | Model card declares MIT. Current release is a 1B-parameter Ettin encoder; F32 weights are 4.11 GB. The repository is currently gated: Hugging Face information must be shared before file access. | Strongest recent research candidate found. Custom scoring script, English, approximately 100+ words. Confirm access conditions and use its own scorer. |

Maker's benchmark reports substantial weaknesses on unseen frontier text and non-native English. |

Official sample explicitly selects CPU when CUDA is absent. Use the published custom classifier definition, not generic autogenerated loading snippets. |
| **[Fast-DetectGPT](https://github.com/baoguangsheng/fast-detect-gpt)** | MIT code; reference-model licenses are separate. | **[Binoculars](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars)** | BSD-3-Clause code; default Falcon reference weights are Apache-2.0. | **[RADAR](https://github.com/IBM/RADAR)** | Apache-2.0 code, but published [RADAR-Vicuna-7B detector weights](https://huggingface.co/TrustSafeAI/RADAR-Vicuna-7B) explicitly restrict use to noncommercial activities. | Local RoBERTa-large classifier, despite the “7B” name referring to its training generator. Research comparison only where restrictions are acceptable. |
| **[Ghostbuster](https://github.com/vivek3141/ghostbuster)** | CC BY 3.0 repository license. Official standalone classifier requires an OpenAI key. | Source available, but the documented workflow sends text to external models. Poor fit for an offline privacy requirement. |

Model-card license declarations are evidence of the publisher's stated terms, not an audit of all training-data rights. Public code, downloadable weights, OSI-style software licensing, and fully reproducible training are different properties. A gated MIT-tagged model also has access conditions to review.

For a ready-made local interface, **[houtini-ai/ai-detect](https://github.com/houtini-ai/ai-detect)** packages Desklib behind an MIT-licensed CLI, Python package, and MCP server, with CPU/CUDA support and a smaller 126 MB ONNX alternative. It describes itself as beta software. It scores sentences separately and combines them, so document-level model benchmarks do not establish the accuracy of that workflow. Its phrasing diagnostics are exploratory heuristics, not proof of AI use. Prefer a direct document-level baseline when evaluating the underlying model. A local MCP server can still return submitted text to a connected hosted assistant; inference location alone does not establish the privacy of the full workflow.

## Accuracy evidence and its limits

**MELD** is a 2026 preprint, revised in September. The authors report 99.24% true-positive rate at 1% false-positive rate across RAID settings, and 99.7% on their held-out MELD-eval pool. Their RAID table is a 2026-09-23 snapshot, not a fresh leaderboard measurement by this review. They explicitly warn that false-positive thresholds do not automatically transfer across domains, registers, or lengths. Scores are not calibrated probabilities of AI authorship. Older MELD releases used different backbones and heads: pin a revision and its matching scorer. These are promising author-reported results requiring independent replication. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06903v2), [release](https://huggingface.co/anon-review-meld-2026/meld).

**Tropa-mini** publishes a useful but vendor-run comparison. At a matched 0.5% overall false-positive rate, it reports 93.2% recall on raw AI, 41.6% on humanized AI, and only 33.6% on its frontier-model set. It also reports a **15.6% false-positive rate on non-native TOEFL essays**. Its headline aggregate performance therefore does not justify applying its threshold universally. English prose of at least 50 words is the stated intended input. The hosted Tropa model is a different system, and its numbers must not be attributed to the open release. [Model card and benchmark](https://huggingface.co/wasitaigeneratedcom/tropa-mini-ai-text-detector).

**Desklib** advertises leading RAID performance at submission, but that is not evidence it remains the best current detector. Its card identifies RAID as training data and provides a custom mean-pooling / single-logit head. Its example truncates at 768 tokens, so feeding a long document once can ignore later text. The GitHub repository contains a README example rather than a complete maintained application. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/desklib/ai-text-detector-v1.01), [official source](https://github.com/desklib/ai-text-detector).

**Fast-DetectGPT** reports AUROC and a large speedup relative to original DetectGPT, measured on research datasets and an A100. AUROC is a ranking metric, not “percentage of documents correctly identified.” Its January 2026 update favors a new reference-model pair, particularly for reasoning-model output; those results should not be assumed for the older default local demo. The experiment's 80 GB A100 is evidence about its environment, not a minimum hardware requirement. [Official README](https://github.com/baoguangsheng/fast-detect-gpt).

**Binoculars** calibrates thresholds for Falcon-7B / Falcon-7B-Instruct at bfloat16 and defaults to observing 512 tokens. Changing reference models, precision, or quantization can invalidate thresholds. Its source supports CPU fallback and two CUDA devices, but does not automatically select Apple's MPS backend. CPU execution being supported says little about acceptable speed. [Implementation](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars/blob/main/binoculars/detector.py), [limitations](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars#limitations).

**RAID** is a useful evaluation framework rather than a detector. Its project currently describes over 10 million documents spanning 11 LLMs, 11 genres, and adversarial transformations. It provides training and evaluation splits; performance on its test set is useful evidence but does not certify coverage of every newer model, language, or writing population. The live JavaScript leaderboard could not be read in this review, so current leaderboard superiority was not independently confirmed. [RAID project](https://github.com/liamdugan/raid).

## Local hardware and privacy

The small encoder classifiers are the sensible first trial on a personal computer. Weights of 0.6–4.1 GB require additional runtime memory for the framework, activations, tokenization, and long documents. File size is not a RAM specification. No credible measured macOS latency or memory figures were verified here.

Binoculars' two approximately 7B models at two bytes per parameter imply roughly **28 GB for weights alone**, before runtime overhead. This is an arithmetic estimate from its default configuration, not a measured minimum. A similar two-8B-model setup is also substantial. The lighter local classifiers avoid that cost. [Binoculars source](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars/blob/main/binoculars/detector.py).

Local inference can keep submitted text on the machine once all required weights and tokenizers are downloaded. Verify this with outbound access disabled and logging inspected before using confidential material. Initial model downloads, hosted demos, cloud scoring variants, and Ghostbuster's API-dependent path have different data boundaries. A localhost GUI does not by itself establish offline inference.

## Hosted and privately deployed commercial options

| Option | Why assess it | Privacy / deployment boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pangram** | First hosted trial candidate based on the independent study below. | Proprietary service. Its [privacy explanation](https://www.pangram.com/data-privacy) says dashboard submissions remain while the account is active, can be deleted from history, and are not used to train its detector. |
| **GPTZero** | Convenient comparison for an established hosted workflow. | Proprietary. [API storage documentation](https://support.gptzero.me/articles/3964919691-are-you-storing-data-from-api-calls) says API submissions are not stored by default; persistence is optional. Dashboard submissions are stored. |
| **Copyleaks** | Candidate when an organization wants supported private deployment. | Proprietary paid offering. [Security documentation](https://docs.copyleaks.com/concepts/security/overview/) explicitly offers on-premises Cloud Private Hubs. Confirm AI-detector scope, hardware, licensing, updates, and outbound connections in a quote. |
| **Originality.ai** | A publishing-oriented suite worth comparing if broader editorial checks matter. | Proprietary hosted candidate; no open-source or self-hosted release was established here. [Product](https://originality.ai/). |

A University of Chicago working paper tested 1,992 human passages across six genres, generations from GPT-4.1, Claude Opus 4 / Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash, and humanized versions. It favored Pangram at low false-positive rates; GPTZero and Originality.ai provide useful comparison points. Its open comparator was an older GPT-2 RoBERTa detector, so it does **not** establish that Pangram beats newer open releases such as MELD or Tropa-mini. [Working paper](https://bfi.uchicago.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/BFI_WP_2025-116.pdf).

## A useful pilot

1. Start with MELD plus ModernBERT-Detect; add Tropa-mini or Desklib for comparison.
2. Use representative human documents, including short writing, non-native English, formulaic prose, and technical writing. Keep threshold calibration documents separate from the final test set.
3. Include current generators and realistic AI editing / paraphrasing, not only untouched generated essays. Test each writing population separately.
4. Measure false-positive rate and recall at an agreed operating point. Keep an inconclusive outcome; do not force every document into a definitive verdict.
5. For confidential inputs, verify offline inference and document logging before the pilot. Pin the model revision, matching code, preprocessing, chunking, and threshold.

For scale: at 1% AI prevalence, a detector with 90% recall and a 1% false-positive rate yields about 90 true flags and 99 false flags per 10,000 documents. Even attractive benchmark metrics can therefore produce more false accusations than correct flags. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a measurement of any listed product.
